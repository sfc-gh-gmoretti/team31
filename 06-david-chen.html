EData & engineering · ground truth

David Chen

Head of Model Risk Engineering

Meeting objective

Get the on-the-ground reality of what's really built and tried, validate the pipeline-consolidation story, and win the engineer. Engineers are where the data actually lives — recognise the real work his team does.

Credibility opener — say first

“Strategy decks are cheap — you and your team are where the risk data actually lives, and you know what's really built, what works, and what's been tried. We'd rather hear the on-the-ground reality from you than assume it, and show you something that ships, not another slide.”

Discovery questions — pick 5–7, lead with ★

Reality

The one takeaway to land

Leave them believing
“Snowflake collapses your fragmented pipeline maintenance — your team ships instead of firefighting.”

What to show: A real pipeline-simplification pattern (dynamic tables / dbt / ingestion); offer a hands-on MVP or hackathon on their actual risk data.

Objections & responses

If they push back…Respond
Strategy talk is cheap — what actually ships?Fair — so let's not talk strategy. We'll show a working pipeline pattern and propose a hands-on MVP on your real data this cycle, with your team in the room.
Another migration just means more work for us.Net reduction, done incrementally alongside what you run today — the point is fewer pipelines to maintain, not a big-bang cutover.

Exit criteria — don't leave without

  • A real pipeline inventory (count + tech)
  • The maintenance-burden share of the week
  • What's been tried to consolidate
  • The biggest class of toil to target
  • Tooling must-keeps — and his buy-in

Out-of-role debrief question (the 5 min after)

“Did we earn an engineer's respect and recognise the real work — or did we sound like another strategy pitch?”
